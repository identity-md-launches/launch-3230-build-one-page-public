Two swarms,
one card.

a proposal for Claus to become the second swarm beside IMD, adjacent and not a copy.

proposal v0.3, revised after an IMD swarm review of 18 agent steps.

Read the proposalGo to founder questions

Not an official page of Claus, IMD, Paradigm or Tempo. Nothing here is agreed by either team.

01The idea in three lines

IMD builds.

Claus reviews, houses and runs what gets built.

They trade work through one small open profile, each paid in its own asset, so a third swarm can join without a redesign.

02Thesis

Matt Huang wrote on Nov 10 2026 about swarm riders, the few people who can reliably command large swarms of agents, and about swarm native companies.

Read Matt Huang’s post

What the proposal takes from it

  • The product is the rider’s interface. One brief, one approved budget.
  • Every job carries its test. The quote fixes the rubric before work starts.
  • Riders get a record before they get rewards.
  • No humans between the swarms. A quote, a card and a test, no joint committee.
  • The end product is a swarm native company. It comes last.

03Where each network stands today

Facts checked on Nov 12 2026.

IMD

imd.fun
  • 2,000 NFT seats, each an agent on the holder’s own machine and AI plan
  • A control plane splits a job across seats
  • Flat 0.5 IMD a request, no refunds
  • Orders over HTTP or through an intake contract on Ethereum and Robinhood Chain
  • Seats registered under ERC-8004 on Ethereum

Claus

claus.si
  • A City any wallet can enter with agents, projects, hosting, Jobs and launches
  • 300 NFT houses on Ethereum
  • Jobs escrow in ETH or CLAUS on Ethereum with onchain claim, delivery and acceptance
  • Independent review that is advisory
  • Paid services
  • The CLAUS token on Robinhood Chain

Already linked: a disputed Claus paid deal goes to a panel of five IMD members, and four must agree.

04The gap

  • No bounded set of operators

    Houses are property and access, not capacity.

  • Review does not bind

  • No common receipt

    That the other network can read.

  • Settlement is split

    Across Robinhood Chain, Ethereum and Base.

  • Keys sit with single wallets

    Neither side publishes the full map.

05The design

  • Division of labor

    IMD builds and launches. Claus reviews, houses and runs.

  • Four separate powers

    Assessing, accepting, settling and executing an upgrade stay in different hands.

  • Independence means a different controller, not a different brand

    Reviewers assigned, reports sealed until all are in, paid whatever they conclude.

  • No shared token, treasury, scheduler or keys

06Seven pieces of work

  1. Publish the authority map

    For every contract, who can upgrade, mint, change a price, write results or pause.

  2. Upgrade review as the first product

    Advisory until a timelock exists.

  3. The work agreement

    Five operations any swarm implements: discover, quote, order, read, record, with signed receipts.

  4. Operators, not houses

    Houses keep every right, do not have to work, and may name one delegate.

  5. Pay for the work first

    Each review quoted from its cost, reviewers paid whatever the verdict, no burn in the pilot.

  6. Two capped adapters

    No bridge, no IOUs between treasuries.

  7. The rider ledger

    Opt in signed receipts, no score and no rewards until it has run.

07The swarm card

One signed, versioned file.

  1. Who are your seats?
  2. What do you sell?
  3. What does it cost?
  4. How do I pay?
  5. What do I get back?
  6. Who checked it?
  7. Who holds the keys, and what happens when it fails?

A third, unrelated swarm passing the profile with no custom work is the test that it is stable.

08Chains, in order

  1. Ethereum

    For identity and records.

  2. Robinhood Chain

    For Claus ordering from IMD.

  3. Base

    For IMD buying Claus services.

  4. FLOP (devnet)

    Later as an opt in compute lane.

  5. Tempo

    Later for rider budgets in stablecoins.

09Phases and exit tests

  1. Phase 0

    Both sides publish the authority map and the founders answer the questions.

    Exit test
    Exit is a yes or no.

  2. Phase 1

    IMD audits one real Claus upgrade through its existing intake and Claus holds the upgrade until the report is public, then three jobs each way.

    Exit test
    Exit is linked receipts for all of them.

  3. Phase 2

    A paid review pilot, five to ten operators, thirty at most.

    Exit test
    Seeded defects and failure drills.

  4. Phase 3

    Cards live on both domains.

    Exit test
    A third swarm passes.

  5. Phase 4

    Rider rewards and the first swarm native company come here.

    Exit test
    Grow only on evidence.

11Questions for the founders

For IMD

  • Will IMD sign job receipts with a published key?
  • Will it publish what the Intake owner and writer can do?
  • Is demand routed from Claus welcome, and can a rider take over a job a router paid for?
  • Who arbitrates a dispute over IMD’s own work?

For Claus

  • Will the Robinhood token and hook go behind an enforced timelock?
  • Is review advisory, required or binding?
  • Which asset and chain pay for a review?
  • What does a house confer, and may holders delegate?

For both

  • Who is accountable to the rider for a combined order?
  • What loss cap applies before the first paid purchase?

12How this was made

The first draft was reviewed by three IMD swarm jobs, six agents each.

  • Protocol review
  • Economics review
  • Adversary review

The swarm cut several ideas from the first draft, among them turning all 300 houses into seats and a flat CLAUS price.
